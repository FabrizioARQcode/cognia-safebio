What is this tool—and what is it not?

Important: CognIA is an educational and research prototype. It is not a clinical test, does not diagnose cognitive impairment, and cannot attribute cognitive changes to AI use.

The results describe your performance on these specific tasks. Scientific conclusions about the effects of AI require a validated protocol, an adequate sample, and controlled experimental conditions.

Why space out learning?

Spaced practice means distributing study across multiple sessions over time instead of concentrating it in a single session. Returning to an idea after an interval requires a person to reconstruct some of the information, which may strengthen their ability to recall it later.

Active recall complements this strategy: instead of rereading passively, we try to retrieve information from memory—for example, by answering questions without looking at our notes. Errors can also be useful when we receive feedback that helps us correct them.

These strategies do not guarantee the same results for everyone or for every task. Their effectiveness depends, among other factors, on the type of content, prior knowledge, difficulty, and the time between sessions.

What you can try to learn more effectively

  • Active recall: close your notes and explain the topic in your own words.
  • Spaced practice: revisit the material the next day and again a week later.
  • Use AI as a tutor: ask for hints and questions before requesting a complete answer.
  • Verify claims: ask for sources, compare evidence, and look for alternative explanations.
  • Practice without assistance: set aside part of each study session to solve problems on your own.
Scientific limitation: a single session cannot determine whether AI caused a change in your memory or learning. Controlled comparisons and follow-up are needed to investigate that question.
